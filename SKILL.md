# Reality Transurfing

Source: Vadim Zeland, *Reality Transurfing: Steps I–V*. This skill extracts the book's frameworks as reusable mental models for reasoning about real decisions — not a book report. Full chapter-by-chapter detail lives in `chapters/`, loaded on demand.

## How to Use This Skill

1. **Diagnose before prescribing.** Read the user's situation against `cheatsheet.md`'s symptom table first — most missteps in applying this framework come from reaching for a technique before identifying which mechanism (pendulum, excess potential, polarization, energy deficit, foreign goal) is actually at play.
2. **Load only what's needed.** For a specific concept, check `glossary.md` first (one-line definitions with chapter pointers). For a technique or step-by-step method, check `patterns.md`. For a deeper dive on a chapter's reasoning, load the specific `chapters/chNN-*.md` file — don't load all 19.
3. **Speak in the book's own terms**, preserving its precise vocabulary (excess potential, outer intention, pendulum, slide, amalgam) rather than diluting it into generic "positive thinking" language — the precision is where the framework's diagnostic power lives.
4. **Ground advice in the actual mechanism**, not vibes: every recommendation should trace to a specific named framework (e.g. "this is excess potential from outer importance — see Ch IV") so the user can verify the reasoning, not just receive a conclusion.

## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

**The Alternatives Space**: an infinite information field containing every possible version of every event — physical reality is one "realized" thread through it. Life unfolds along **life lines** (sectors of consistent scenery/script); a person's *own thought-energy* determines which life line they occupy, and shifts between lines happen when that energy changes. This is the substrate every other framework operates on.

**Excess Potential → Balanced Forces**: attaching disproportionate meaning (importance, desire, fear, idealization) to anything creates "excess potential" — a tension in the energy field. **Balanced Forces** arise to eliminate that tension, usually by producing the *opposite* of what was wanted. This is the single most load-bearing mechanism in the book: most "bad luck," backlash, and self-sabotage traces back to it. The fix is never more force — it's reducing the attached importance.

**Pendulums**: autonomous energy-structures formed when groups of people think alike (crowds, fandoms, institutions, addictions). They grow by extracting adherents' emotional energy (indignation, fear, worship) and, once established, dictate behavior ("Do as I do!"). You cannot defeat a pendulum by fighting it — only by disengaging attention or redirecting the energy exchange via a present-tense **Declaration of Intention**.

**Inner Intention vs. Outer Intention**: inner intention is the resolve to *act* — direct effort, willpower, forcing outcomes; it only works on things you've made "attributes" of a controllable game. Outer intention is the resolve to *have* — formed in unity of heart and mind, it shapes reality without direct effort and is the source of what looks like "paranormal" luck. Confusing the two (trying to *force* outer-intention-style results) is a recurring failure mode.

**The Target Slide**: a deliberately held mental image of a goal *as already achieved*, lived from the inside rather than watched externally. Systematic, low-effort visualization of the slide (via the frame, energy currents, scenery reduction, or several other techniques in `patterns.md`) is the primary mechanism for materializing a goal — paired with ordinary real-world action on it, never a substitute for that action.

**The Dual Mirror & Seven Mirror Principles**: reality is a mirror reflecting your relationship to it, with a **response delay**. Its most consequential rule: the mirror reflects *content*, not *orientation* — meaning fear/hatred materializes exactly as reliably as desire, since both fix attention on the same object. The practical fix is redirecting attention from the unwanted reflection to a deliberately constructed positive image, then releasing the grip and letting the delayed reflection catch up (never anxiously checking).

**Goals vs. Doors, Foreign vs. Innermost Goals**: a person's true goal produces genuine desire, not duty; pendulum-imposed "foreign goals" (chased for prestige or others' approval) produce tension instead. A **door** is the walkable path to the innermost goal — often not the one that looks most impressive.

**Energy of Intention**: free energy (distinct from physiological energy) powers all of the above; it's drained by unrealized obligations ("plummets"), toxin buildup, and chronic importance — and restored by dropping/realizing plummets, energy-channel exercises, and physical cleansing, not by "trying harder."

**The Maker's Verdict**: the book's final, most totalizing instrument — the unconditional right to declare your own truth/value for your own reality, held without hedging, guilt, or need for external validation. Presented as the actual gate the whole framework opens onto.

## Scope & Limits

- This is one author's speculative model, explicitly self-relativized in the Ch XIX Afterword — treat it as a reasoning scaffold for the user's situation, not literal metaphysics to defend.
- Do not use this skill to construct advice aimed at controlling or harming another person's outcomes — the book itself is explicit that outer intention reliably shapes only the practitioner's own "world layer," not other people's (see Vanga case study, Ch XVIII).
- Not a substitute for real-world action, professional/medical/financial/legal advice, or mental-health support — frameworks like "the Healing game" (Ch XI) are the author's speculative reframes, not medical claims.
