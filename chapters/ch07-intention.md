# Chapter VII: Intention

## Core Idea
The Guardian's riddle from Chapter I is answered here: what's actually realized in the alternatives space is never mere *desire* — it's **intention**, specifically the rare state of **outer intention** (unity of heart and mind, free of desire and importance), which "chooses" a life line rather than fighting to create it; this is the theoretical center of the entire Transurfing method.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Desire vs. Inner Intention vs. Outer Intention** (the book's central three-way distinction):
  - **Desire**: attention concentrated on the *goal itself*, with the possibility of non-fulfillment built in — desire inherently creates excess potential and has no power of its own ("desire fails even to lift a finger").
  - **Inner intention**: attention concentrated on the *process of moving toward* the goal — "resoluteness in the decision to act." This works, but costs enormous energy because it directly pressures the external world ("I insist that...").
  - **Outer intention**: attention concentrated on *how the goal realizes itself independently* — "resoluteness in the decision to have." Rather than acting on the world, it *selects* which already-existing alternative becomes physical reality ("Circumstances are such that...", "It turns out that...").
  - When to use: whenever effortful "trying harder" (inner intention) is producing diminishing or opposite returns — the shift to outer intention is choosing rather than changing.
  - How: outer intention arises specifically from **unity between heart and mind** — when both agree (consciously and subconsciously) on an outcome, free of doubt and importance, the corresponding life line manifests, seemingly independent of visible effort.
- **The Purification of Intention (removing desire from intention)**: a concrete technique — explicitly accept the possibility of failure *once*, at the outset, with a real "safety net" thought-through, then stop revisiting failure and simply proceed toward the goal the way you'd walk to a newsstand to buy a paper.
  - How: run the failure scenario through your mind once, decide what Plan B would be, accept "this wouldn't be the end of the world" — this removes the excess potential (doubt + importance) that keeps outer intention from activating.
- **The Guardian / Conscious Awareness as the master skill**: neither the needy, indignant, nor fighter (Ch VI) but the "awake," detached witness ("Guardian") is Transurfing's answer to who is master of their own fate — control is exercised over *your own awareness*, not over the world directly.

## Key Concepts
- **Lucid dreaming** (used throughout as the working model for how intention functions free of "material density"): once aware you're dreaming, the *only* two conditions for full control are (1) knowing it's a dream and (2) knowing anything is possible there — flight, transformation, anything, is achieved purely through intention, not effortful desire.
- **The dream-space hypothesis**: dreams are not imagination but the subconscious ("heart/soul") directly accessing unrealized (or sometimes realized) sectors of the alternatives space — this is why dream imagery can be more vivid than anything the brain could synthesize from memory alone, and why dream figures/settings resemble-but-differ from waking reality.
- **The risk of lucid dreaming**: a caution (not a technique) — the soul could theoretically wander into a *materialized* sector of the alternatives space and fail to return, which the book speculatively connects to unexplained disappearances; framed as a genuine caveat, not a scare tactic, with the advice to trust a negative gut feeling about practicing it.
- **Why fears/negative expectations manifest so reliably**: because heart and mind rarely agree on anything as completely and doubt-free as what they both fear — that rare full agreement (even on something unwanted) is precisely the condition that activates outer intention, "green-lighting" the feared outcome regardless of personal will.
- **The vaudeville life line**: a life line on which any problem, however complex it initially seems, resolves itself easily or absurdly — reachable by deciding, with genuine detachment (not denial), to treat an incoming problem as trivial before even hearing its details.

## Mental Models
- **The fly and the open window (revisited)**: inner intention is the fly banging against the closed pane; outer intention is discovering — not forcing — the open window right beside it. Outer intention doesn't create a new exit; it selects the alternative where the exit was already there.
- **"You choose the pencil's position, you don't move the pencil"**: outer intention (as in telekinesis) doesn't physically move an object — it shifts *which sector* of the alternatives space (where the pencil is already located differently) becomes materialized. Same logical structure as choosing a life line for yourself.
- **Falling from a roof / giving yourself up to gravity**: inner intention is "running until you drop" (forcing an outcome through effort); outer intention is stepping off the edge and letting gravity (the already-existing pull toward the goal) do the work — the goal is "already decided," not achieved through struggle.
- **The kitten and the mirror**: a kitten's mood shifts fluidly between playful and defensive as it reacts to its own reflection — illustrating how, in non-lucid dreaming (and in un-self-aware waking life), the "script" is continuously and unconsciously self-corrected based on the observer's own shifting expectations, without recognizing this is happening.

## Anti-patterns
- **Frantic, desperate wanting** ("I have to have this, whatever it takes"): the intensity of desire itself signals disbelief that the goal is achievable, which increases doubt and excess potential — paradoxically making the outcome less likely, not more.
- **Asking, praying, or demanding from "higher powers"**: requests and complaints create excess potential (you're stating you lack something) rather than choosing; the book explicitly frames gratitude — not petition — as the energetically constructive counterpart.
- **Trying to force outer intention through willpower ("inner intention to acquire outer intention")**: a logical trap — outer intention specifically cannot be summoned by effortful insistence, since insistence *is* inner intention; it only emerges from genuine heart-mind unity, which importance and doubt block.
- **Constantly revisiting the "what if I fail" scenario**: the failure-acceptance exercise is meant to be done *once*; looping on it repeatedly recreates the very doubt/importance it was meant to dissolve.
- **Trying to seize control by force once "awake" (in a dream or in life)**: stepping into the observer role and then trying to dominate all outcomes by sheer will collapses back into inner intention and destroys balance — genuine awareness is about observation, not micromanagement.

## Worked Example
The chapter's clearest worked demonstration is the **newsstand comparison**, used repeatedly to anchor the abstract concept: buying a newspaper involves no felt "desire," no doubt, no excess potential — you simply go and get it, with total (if mundane) confidence it will happen, and if it somehow doesn't (the shop is closed), it's a minor, forgettable inconvenience, not a crisis. This is contrasted against high-stakes "I must achieve X" goals, which generate desire, doubt, fear of failure, and therefore excess potential and diminished odds. The prescribed **purification technique** applies the newsstand-level nonchalance to a genuinely important goal: (1) state the goal, (2) consciously run through the failure scenario *once*, identifying a real fallback/safety net, (3) accept "if this fails, it's not the end of the world," (4) stop thinking about success or failure and simply move toward the goal with the same flat confidence as walking to buy a paper.

## Key Takeaways
1. What gets realized is never mere desire — it's intention, and specifically the rare state of *outer* intention (heart-mind unity, free of doubt and importance) that "chooses" rather than "fights for" an outcome.
2. Inner intention (effortful action toward a goal) works but is energy-expensive; outer intention (letting the goal's realization proceed on its own, having already "decided" it will happen) is the higher-leverage mechanism Transurfing is built around.
3. Fears and worst expectations are realized so reliably precisely because heart and mind agree on them completely and without doubt — the same mechanism that could serve a desired goal, working in reverse.
4. The practical purification technique: accept the possibility of failure once (with a real safety net), then stop thinking about success/failure and act with newsstand-level nonchalance.
5. The Guardian (detached, aware witness) is Transurfing's answer to "what role should I play in my own life" — not needy, not indignant, not a fighter, but an awake observer who steers lightly.
6. Genuine control comes from *awareness*, not from seizing the reins and forcing outcomes — trying to dominate the script once "awake" collapses back into inner intention and its costs.
