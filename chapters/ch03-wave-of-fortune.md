# Chapter III: The Wave of Fortune

## Core Idea
Unlike a pendulum, the wave of fortune is an energetic structure that does *not* feed on your energy — it's simply the set of life lines favorable to you, and you attune to it (or lose it) purely through the quality — not the intensity — of your thoughts: accept and radiate positive energy, and good circumstances cascade; take in or radiate negative energy, and your "boomerang" comes back as disappointment.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Wave of Fortune**: a stationary accumulation of favorable, "golden vein" life lines in the alternatives space. Riding it costs no energy and requires no struggle — only not pushing it away.
  - When to use: as the positive counterpart to pendulum-avoidance — the goal isn't merely "stop feeding pendulums," it's "actively attune to the wave."
  - How: notice small good news, savor and dwell on it (rather than forgetting it out of habit) — this is what lets one piece of luck cascade into a run of luck, since adjacent favorable life lines cluster together like a "golden vein."
- **The Boomerang Principle**: thoughts — positive or negative — return to you as matching circumstances; the same mechanism that lets pendulums hurt you (§Ch II) is what lets deliberate positive attention help you.
  - How: don't just avoid negative energy input; also stop *transmitting* negative energy outward (complaining, shouting, dwelling on dislikes) — both directions matter equally.
- **Transmission (become a positive-energy broadcaster, not just receiver)**: actively holding and savoring good news, gratitude, and anticipated improvement keeps your thought-energy vibration matched to favorable life lines.

## Key Concepts
- **Golden vein**: a cluster of adjacent life lines in the alternatives space that share favorable circumstances — catching hold of one lucky break slides you toward more of the same, by inertia, unless a destructive pendulum intercepts you.
- **Magic ritual**: any personal ritual (discounting the first customer of the day, rubbing money on merchandise) that works not through inherent power but by helping the practitioner subconsciously attune their thought energy to the frequency of a successful life line — "it works as a theatrical requisite, not as a power unto itself."
- **Remembering**: the practiced habit of catching yourself mid-negative-reaction and consciously switching attitude — the mechanism by which pendulum-defeat (Ch II) and wave-riding (this chapter) become durable rather than one-off.

## Mental Models
- **Wave carrying an exhausted swimmer to shore**: unlike a pendulum (which pulls energy from you), the wave of fortune only carries you if you let it — it has no investment in you personally and will pass by without malice if ignored.
- **"Finders keepers, losers weepers"**: the wave doesn't chase you; you simply have to not actively push it away through negativity, the way one "chases off" a bluebird by not driving it away rather than by grasping at it.
- **Forest of flowers and thorns**: information/news is a mixed forest — deliberately choose to pick up (dwell on) the fragrant flowers (good news) rather than the poisonous thorns (bad news), since either choice determines what grows in your life.

## Anti-patterns
- **Complaining about your current circumstances while anticipating a future fix** ("I hate this house, but I'll love the new one!"): the negative vibration attached to your *current* state follows you and reattaches to the new circumstance — the new house/job/trip inherits the same complaint-energy, producing the same disappointments.
- **Forgetting good news quickly** while dwelling on bad news: bad news naturally produces a stronger, longer emotional reaction (it signals threat), so without deliberate practice, attention defaults toward negativity — this asymmetry has to be corrected on purpose.
- **Habitually seeking out problems rather than solutions**: people who default to criticism and "what could go wrong" keep summoning more of exactly that, regardless of whether they're "good" or "bad" people — they're simply hooked by a destructive pendulum's habitual-negativity mechanism.
- **Treating any outcome that deviates from your original plan as automatically "bad luck"**: a missed flight that turns out to be a crashed plane, or a "wasted" opportunity that didn't fit your plan, shows that judging events against a fixed script blinds you to which life line you're actually on.

## Worked Example
The chapter walks through the "house" scenario in full: a person who hates their current home and fixates on escaping it will, upon moving, find the new home riddled with equivalent problems (leaking taps, peeling paint, bad neighbors) — because the negative vibration, not the physical building, is what's being carried forward. The corrective technique is spelled out in two explicit steps: (1) accept the current situation and actively find things to be grateful for in it (even a disliked house "keeps you warm and dry" — thank it); (2) *then* start deliberately imagining the desired outcome (browse home listings, look at furniture, hold the image of the wanted house) — gratitude for what is, plus attention on what's wanted, together shift the vibration; complaining about the present while merely wishing for the future does not.

## Key Takeaways
1. The wave of fortune requires no fighting or effort — only not repelling it through negativity; this is qualitatively different from "working hard for luck."
2. Your thoughts function as a boomerang in both directions: what you take in (react to) and what you put out (express) both return as matching life circumstances.
3. Good news should be deliberately savored and revisited, not let slip by — this is the practical technique for staying on a "golden vein" of favorable life lines.
4. Judging any given moment as good/bad only by whether it matched your original expectation blinds you to real shifts in fortune (missed flight/crashed plane).
5. "Remembering" — catching yourself reacting out of habit and consciously choosing differently — is a trainable skill, not a personality trait; expect early relapses and treat them as part of the practice, not failure.

## Connects To
- **Ch II (Pendulums)**: the wave of fortune is presented as pendulums' structural opposite — no energy cost, no self-interest in you, easy to lose because it doesn't fight to keep you.
- **Ch IV (Balance)**: over-attachment even to *good* outcomes (excess importance) can trigger balancing forces — the wave should be ridden lightly, not clutched.
- **Ch VIII (Slides)**: the deliberate practice of "holding a desired image in mind" (imagining the house you want) is the seed of the fuller "slide" technique developed later.
