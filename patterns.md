# Patterns & Techniques — Reality Transurfing

Step-by-step methods extracted from the book, organized by what they're for.

## Diagnosing a Problem

**The Foreign-Goal Diagnostic Checklist** (Ch X): before chasing a goal, check — do you want the goal itself or only its *attributes* (status, salary, prestige)? Does pursuing it feel like tension/duty rather than genuine desire? Would you still want it if no one else could see you achieve it? If it fails these checks, it's likely a pendulum-imposed foreign goal, not your true door.

**Polarization Check** (Ch XVI, XIX): when a relationship or situation feels cursed with recurring friction (bad neighbors, a resistant child, chronic conflict), ask whether you've built a *comparative* judgment ("I'm right, they're wrong") rather than a neutral one. If yes, the friction is balanced forces responding to polarization, not the other person's inherent nature.

**Excess-Potential Scan** (Ch IV): notice where you've attached disproportionate meaning — to an outcome, a possession, your own image, a fear. The intensity of your reaction is a direct measure of the excess potential you're carrying, and thus of how strongly balanced forces will react.

## Reducing Excess Potential / Freeing Energy

**Drop the Plummets** (Ch XIX): list every unrealized obligation, promise, or "someday" plan you're carrying. For each: either commit to actually doing it, or consciously release it. Half-finished intentions are the single largest energy drain.

**Stop Justifying Yourself** (guilt cure, Ch XIX): rather than arguing yourself out of guilt, simply stop explaining/defending your actions except when strictly necessary. The external behavior change (silence under scrutiny) causes the internal guilt to fade — not the reverse.

**Stop Proving Your Worth** (inferiority cure, Ch XIX): consciously give up all attempts to demonstrate your significance or correct others' underestimation of you. As external proof-seeking stops, self-worth stabilizes on its own — the same feedback-chain logic as the guilt cure.

**Accept Failure in Advance** (doubt/fear cure, Ch XIX, XVIII): before a high-stakes moment, tell yourself "if this fails, maybe that's actually best for me." This removes the desperate charge from the outcome without faking indifference to the goal itself.

## Working the Target Slide

**The Frame** (Ch XIX): pick one compressed, characteristic flash-image from inside your achieved goal (e.g. "shaking hands on the signed deal"). Give it a short mental label. Switch it on briefly and often, without effort.

**The Explosion Wave** (Ch XIX): immediately after forming the frame, imagine a sphere of energy exploding outward from your body in all directions — "sending the thought into the universe."

**Energy Currents / "Turning the Key"** (Ch XIX, XI): imagine two arrows exiting the solar plexus; mentally turn them so the front points up and the rear points down, activating rising/descending currents. Hold this alongside the target slide, ideally on a quiet walk.

**The Outer Sphere** (Ch XIX): feel an external membrane roughly 5–7 meters around you that *pulls you* rather than being pushed by your will — a felt conversion from inner intention (forcing objects) to outer intention (letting the world move you).

**Scenery Reduction** (Ch XIX): project the target slide over ordinary surroundings — look at a park bench as if it were the garden of your future house. Reduce incidental thoughts back to the goal's common denominator throughout the day.

**The Goal Amalgam** (Ch XIX, "for the lazy"): declare "the *how* is not my concern" and hand the mechanics to outer intention entirely. Revisit the slide only for pleasure, never as a scheduled obligation — forcing it defeats the technique.

## Interpersonal Techniques

**Frailing** (Ch XII): replace the intention to *get* something from a person with the intention to *give* — attention, appreciation, a small favor — without conditions attached. This engages the other person's own inner intention on your behalf.

**Correcting the Mirror in Relationships** (Ch XIX): when someone (a child, a partner) resists control, look for where you've "over-tightened the spokes" (excess coercion, rigid demands) and deliberately loosen elsewhere — offer a framed choice ("wash dishes or go to the shops?") instead of a bare command; use humor/shared mischief to cut tension.

**The Guardian Angel Practice** (Ch IX): address your own "fraile"/intuition in a warm, second-person internal voice (as if comforting a child), rather than issuing commands to yourself — softens the heart-mind split enough for outer intention to engage.

## Handling Pendulums

**Declaration of Intention** (Ch XVI): during any pendulum-mediated exchange (a drink, a cigarette, a routine task) that you can't avoid, silently affirm your goal in the *present tense* ("everything is going wonderfully") rather than drifting into worry — this redirects the borrowed energy credit toward your own goal instead of the pendulum's.

**Disengage, Don't Fight** (Ch II, XVI): a pendulum cannot be defeated by direct opposition (you can't outrun your own shadow) — the only two effective moves are withdrawing attention entirely, or redirecting the energy exchange via declaration of intention.

## The Transaction (Deliberate Life-Line Shift)

**Turn the Key → Visualize → Clear Sight** (Ch XIV): (1) perform a small felt "turning" gesture as a trigger; (2) hold a clear, brief visualization of the desired shift; (3) consciously let go of doubt/expectation immediately after, rather than checking for results.

## Maintaining Favorable Coordination

**Treat Any Reflection as Positive** (Ch XIII, XVIII): at each ambiguous fork (an unexpected delay, a "bad" piece of news), consciously interpret it as favorable before its outcome is confirmed. This is a standing practice, not a one-time technique — repeated at every fork, not just big decisions.

**The Amalgam as Background Practice** (Ch XVIII, XIX): pick one dominant formula ("my world takes care of me") and cite it at *every* occasion, especially minor/negative ones — the repetition, not any single dramatic use, is what establishes the tone shift.
